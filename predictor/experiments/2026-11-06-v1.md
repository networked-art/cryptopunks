# V1 price investigation

Baseline: `04d4fc4775f8164054c0d4b7bdde0690e8f1dc08`.

## Diagnosis and fixed candidate

The previous cycle's ten observed V1 sales include nine sales of Punk 2756 and
one of Punk 9013. This is not ten independent examples. Nine 2756 forecasts
equal exactly 85% of the contemporaneous V1 asking floor, even while executed
sales are much lower. For example, an 8.75 ETH asking floor forced a 7.4375 ETH
valuation before a 2.1 ETH sale.

The code treats the lowest unsold ask as a minimum fair value. An asking price
does not establish that a buyer will pay it. The fixed candidate removes only
that hard minimum from `predict_v1`. Keep the multiplier, rejected-bid evidence,
V2 price model, probability models, and promotion policy unchanged.

## Validation plan

Record before extracting the additional archived evidence. Reconstruct every
available historical V1 forecast from its contemporaneous archived V2 prediction,
market multiplier, floor, and reservation signal. Require the incumbent replay
to match the actual served forecast before grading a counterfactual.

Apply the same fixed deletion to each chronological archived forecast. Do not
retune parameters using these outcomes. Report all available sales, distinct
Punks, dates, affected sales, median percentage error, value-weighted error,
interval coverage, and individual regressions. Also report the earliest half
and latest half of sale timestamps separately. The previously inspected 90-day
outcomes are diagnostic evidence, not a fresh statistical holdout.

Accept this structural correction only if median percentage error and
value-weighted error both improve by at least 10% overall, both improve in the
two chronological halves, no replayed sale's absolute error increases, and the
replay matches all incumbent prices. Report concentration by Punk rather than
claiming broad calibration from repeated trades. If the correction does not
meet these checks, keep the incumbent model.

Test output behavior when asks are above realized value, and preserve active
bid and rejected-bid behavior. Validate output invariants over all 20,000 active
predictions using the same fixed inputs. V2 predictions must remain identical.

Push validated changes on a `codex/` branch and open a PR. GitHub requires one
approval. Merge and deploy only after that requirement is met, with no admin
bypass. Record the previous image and active run before any deployment.

## Candidate A rejected

The exact archived replay covers 17 V1 sales of five distinct Punks. Market
table multipliers are rounded to six decimals; the forecast drivers preserve
the full precision needed to reproduce the archived output exactly.

Deleting the asking-floor minimum reduces overall median error from 158.70% to
104.26% and value-weighted error from 41.80% to 36.82%. However, it worsens three
earlier sales and does not meet the chronological checks. Keep the minimum for
now. This candidate's report is `2026-11-06-v1-floor-deletion.json`.

## Candidate B: separate collection liquidity from rare-type premiums

A second root cause is independent of the asking-floor minimum. An Alien sold
for 180 ETH in July. When the 30-day V1 sample contained that Alien and a human
Punk sold for 2.2 ETH, their median was 91.1 ETH. Dividing this by the V2 market
median raised the collection-wide multiplier to 0.5756, despite a V1 asking
floor of 2.23 ETH against V2's 32.26 ETH. The next two forecasts for human Punk
3532 were 18.57 ETH before sales at 3 ETH.

Keep rare-type premiums in the V2 per-Punk model. Compute the collection
liquidity sale ratio from human Punks on both sides, using immutable normalized
type traits. Keep the existing minimum sample requirement, weighting, floor,
bid evidence, and asking-floor minimum. There are no new fitted parameters.

Before replaying B, require at least a 10% improvement in both overall median
percentage error and value-weighted error, no increase in any replayed sale's
absolute error, exact incumbent reproduction, and unchanged V2 output. Report
earlier and later periods separately. The bug is activated by rare-type sales,
so periods without that trigger need not improve but must not regress. These
are tests of a fixed correction on known historical data, not an unseen
statistical holdout or evidence that all V1 prices are calibrated.

## Candidate B accepted

| Evaluation | Sales / Punks | Median percentage error, old to new | Value-weighted error, old to new |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All archived V1 sales | 17 / 5 | 158.70% to 93.18% | 41.80% to 28.49% |
| Earlier timestamps | 8 / 4 | 140.96% to 32.28% | 32.86% to 18.17% |
| Later timestamps | 9 / 2 | 158.70% to 158.70% | 128.21% to 128.21% |

The change passes B's acceptance criteria. Two forecasts for Punk 3532 change
from 18.5678 ETH to 1.8955 ETH before 3 ETH sales. No other replayed price changes,
and no individual sale's absolute price error increases. Interval coverage stays
at 23.53%; this correction does not establish calibrated V1 intervals. The
remaining errors, including the asking-floor issue, remain substantial.

Both raw sale counts and the original multiplier are reconstructed exactly at
each historical run's data cutoff. Rare trades remain in reported total volume
and per-Punk model history, but not in the human market liquidity sale cohort.
The JSON report includes cohort membership counts for the current full run.

## Validation and reproduction

44 tests pass. The new regression case fails against the original market
context function, and passes for Alien, Ape, and Zombie sales after the fix.
Package and model version advance to 0.1.1 with no dependency-version changes.
`uv lock --check` and `uv sync --frozen` pass.

Both complete models were trained locally on the same frozen snapshot without
database writes. All 20,000 candidate predictions have valid prices, ordered
intervals, finite probabilities, and unique standard/Punk identities. Every V2
prediction is identical, including its drivers and probability output. The
current archived V1 predictions are also unchanged: no recent V1 rare sale is
currently activating the bug. This fix prevents the observed failure recurring.
